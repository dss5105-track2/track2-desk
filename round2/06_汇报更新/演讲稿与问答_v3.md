## 讲稿（约 600 词，280 秒，留 20 秒换页）

方括号里是建议用时。数字都对应页面上的数字，别临场改。

**0 · 封面** [10 s]
Good evening, we are Group NN. Our project is the Subcontracting Desk: it turns the chat message that sends a batch to an outside workshop into a verified, recorded decision.

**1 · The dispatch problem** [35 s]
Today that decision is a chat message. Behind it are three questions: can the workshop make this product, can it take the batch now, is it allowed to? Get one wrong and the batch bounces back a day later; in a ten-day cycle that is the margin gone. So the desk reads the chat, decides on numbers, and leaves a record. The value: a verified recommendation for the dispatcher, fewer late batches for the factory, a date the customer can rely on.

**2 · Solution and architecture** [50 s]
The language model only reads: order, pieces, date, constraint. Then code takes over, with three mechanisms. One, the text decides: every extracted value is checked against the message, and anything the message does not contain is dropped. Two, eligibility before ranking: three hard checks, then two dates per eligible shop, expected and if reworked, and only then a ranking by the objective. Three, traced, then booked: every number in the explanation comes from a tool output, and nothing is booked until the dispatcher confirms. The bottom row is one real message going through: the standing constraint recorded, four shops eligible, GiantWeave on time even if reworked.

**3 · Why minimise late deliveries** [40 s]
Our primary objective is to minimise late deliveries: the share of batches returned after their due date. Three reasons. Relevant: the due date is the one constraint every order carries, and quick response exists to protect it. Measurable: the simulator records when every batch returns. Controllable: lateness depends on queue, capacity and transport, exactly what the choice of workshop changes. The other objectives stay: defects and fairness as switchable settings, one hybrid, and robustness under the shock scenario. And we declare our tie-break up front: equal lateness, shorter turnaround.

**4 · What we have built and tested** [60 s]
The loop runs end to end, and the first results are in. In simulation, on the official 120 orders, random allocation is 34 percent late. Our lateness setting is 1.7 percent, the same as a simple earliest-finish rule with no language model; we do not hide that tie. On 200 fresh order books the simple rule never saw, we are ahead: 0.9 against 1.7 percent, better on 72 of 100 sets. Where the simple rule still wins: it is a day faster, and it edges us in the shock run by one batch in ten. Beyond the allocator: 56 tests pass, every number in every reply traces to a tool output, all six of us labelled the 30 messages and froze the labels, and we measured the language model before trusting it: GPT-5 mini now reads all 30 official and 38 new messages right; the cheapest model started at 86.

**5 · Risks, assumptions and limitations** [45 s]
Three risks. No clean language test yet: the 30 messages were used in development, the 38 new ones are AI-written; so four teammates who never touch the prompt are writing a held-out inbox, answers frozen before the first run. The simulator is kinder than reality: a seven-day week, and the estimator does not know when a workshop closes; we report a Sunday variant and will add closed-workshop detection. And our policy is not optimal: a hindsight search reaches zero late, but a dispatcher cannot see future orders. Our assumptions are on the bottom line.

**6 · Roadmap and success criteria** [40 s]
Next: the held-out inbox, frozen before the first run. Closed-workshop detection, so the system notices a stuck queue. A user test with students from other groups, a public demo, and an evaluation draft with ten failure cases. Our criteria: under 2 percent late on the official orders, normal and shock; fewer late batches than the simple rule on fresh data; 90 percent right behaviour on the held-out inbox; and every promised rework date kept. Thank you.

## 问答预案

老师问答 2 分钟，大约 3 到 4 个问题。每条先给中文的问题，再给英文的答法，最后标可以翻的备用页。按被问到的概率排序。

**1. 为什么选迟交，不选次品或公平？**
Three reasons. The due date is the one constraint every order carries, and quick response exists to protect it. The simulator measures it directly, so we optimise what we can measure. And the choice of workshop is exactly what moves it: queue, capacity, transport. Defects and fairness are not dropped; they are settings of the same allocator, reported on the same 120 orders, plus one hybrid. (第 3 页)

**2. 你们和最快规则打平了，贡献是什么？**
On the official orders we tie on late batches and halve the late days. On 200 fresh order books the rule was never shaped on, we are ahead: better on 72 of 100 jittered sets, worse on 3, and ahead again with tighter due dates, extra accessory orders and Sundays off. And the desk adds what no allocator has: it reads the chat, refuses, quotes two dates and leaves an audit record. We also say where the simple rule wins: it is a day faster. (附录 E)

**3. 为什么不用迟交率 1.0% 的那个设置？**
We tested that, paired, on the same data and seeds. Its edge holds only on the official orders, 8 of 10 seeds; on 200 fresh order books the two settings are indistinguishable on lateness, with p above 0.05. The robust differences are elsewhere: it returns batches five days later and costs 9 percent less. The track names turnaround as the business impact, so we declared the faster tie-break and keep the other as a cost-saving setting. (附录 F)

**4. 你们怎么知道语言模型能泛化？**
We do not yet; that is our first risk. The 30 official messages were used in development, and the 38 new ones are AI-written and served as a smoke test, 97 to 100 percent. The real test is a held-out inbox of 44 messages written by four teammates who never touch the prompt, with answers frozen before the first run. We will report the first score as it comes, high or low. (第 5 页)

**5. 车间突然关门怎么办？**
Today the system does not notice. In the shock run, where one workshop closes for two weeks, we are at 2.0 percent late, the simple rule at 1.9. Sprint 2 adds detection from a stuck queue: once detected, the shop receives no new batches and waiting ones are rerouted. The acceptance criterion is on slide 6: shock lateness no worse than the normal run. (第 6 页)

**6. 除了调一个大模型加几条规则，技术贡献在哪？**
Three mechanisms. The text decides: every extracted value is checked against the message, so the model cannot invent an order number or a quantity; that took the cheapest model from 86 to 100 percent on the official messages. Eligibility before ranking, with two dates per shop including the rework case: 100 percent of those dates held in 1,200 simulated batches, against 94 percent for a single expected date. And traced explanations with confirm-then-book: every number in every reply comes from a tool output. (第 2 页，附录 D)

**7. 混合目标的权重怎么定的？阈值有依据吗？**
We scaled each metric to 0–1 within the candidate set first, then scanned the defect and load weights on 50 seeds. We chose the knee of the lateness–defect curve: up to a defect weight of 0.25, one more late batch buys several hundred fewer defective pieces; beyond it, 17 points of lateness per point of defects. The load cap of 30 percent is the largest shop's capacity share, 28.6 percent, rounded. Checked on held-out seeds. The knee was found on the official orders; re-checking it on fresh order sets is in Sprint 2. (第 3 页)

**8. 你们的标签是自己定的，老师的标准可能不同？**
Yes, and we say so. All six of us labelled the 30 messages independently, kappa 0.70. Twenty-five followed the majority; five below five-of-six agreement were settled by one stated rule: if the named workshop cannot take the job, refuse and offer an alternative, even when the message is a question. The counts per kind then match the instructor's published mix exactly. (第 4 页)

**9. 为什么用 GPT-5 mini 不用更便宜的 nano？**
We measured both. After our code checks, nano reads all 30 official messages right but still misreads one new phrasing, a date “sooner than planned, not said when”, three runs out of three. Mini gets all 30 and all 38 right, at about 3.6 US cents per 30 messages, four times nano's price. The whole evaluation so far cost 1.3 dollars. (附录 C)

**10. “在模拟中迟交 1.7%”和真实工厂有什么关系？**
It is a simulation number, and we label it as one: 34 percent is random allocation, not the factory's history. What carries over is the mechanism, three checks and two dates before a batch leaves. The gap between simulation and reality is our second risk: we report a Sunday variant separately, and the estimator does not yet know when a workshop closes.

**11. 事后搜索能做到 0% 迟交，为什么不用那个方法？**
Because it knows every order in advance. A dispatcher only sees the orders that have arrived; our policy decides online, one batch at a time. The hindsight number tells us how much room is left, not how to get there.

**12. 为什么不做多智能体，让车间互相竞价？**
Every number a workshop would “bid” is already on its profile card, so a negotiation would recompute the same estimates with more cost and less determinism. The track says the mechanism is not graded and that a well-tooled dispatcher beats a transcript. We spend the time on verification instead.

**13. 消息按时间顺序处理、确认后占产能，这是你们的假设？**
Yes, a team decision. It matters: the instructor's published counts per kind match when each message is read on its own with the morning queues; processed in order, two of the thirteen on-time requests end up a day late because earlier confirmations took the capacity. We use the morning queues for the labels and the ledger at that moment for the decisions, and say so.

**14. 一键确认会不会让人不看就点？**
It only takes recommendations that stay on time even if the batch is reworked, and it holds back any request that shares a workshop with a tight one. On the morning inbox that is 7 of 13; the other 6 are left for the dispatcher, tightest first. Whether people trust it is exactly what the user test in Sprint 2 will tell us.

**15. 分工序扩展（--stages）为什么不做？**
It is optional and the default track does not need it. We checked what it would change: with stage capabilities, our fastest flexible shop can take only 33 of the 120 orders, so every number would move. Our eligibility check is a list of questions and the fourth one is a small change; we will assess it after Sprint 2, once the verification work is done.
