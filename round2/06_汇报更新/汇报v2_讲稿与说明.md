## 讲稿（624 词，约 4 分 30 秒到 4 分 50 秒）

每页开头方括号里是建议用时，合计 4 分 15 秒，留 45 秒余量。讲稿也在 pptx 每页的备注里。

**1 · 封面** [10 s]
Good evening, we are Group NN. Our project is the Subcontracting Desk: it turns the chat message that sends a batch to an outside workshop into a checked, recorded allocation.

**2 · 问题** [40 s]
Today the decision is a chat message. Behind it are three questions: can the workshop make the product, can it take the batch now, is it allowed to? Get one wrong and the batch bounces back a day later. Our desk checks all three and recommends one workshop, with the reason.
This is a real message, R12: nothing new to QuickStitch this week. The desk records that, gives two dates per shop, expected and if reworked, and books nothing until the dispatcher confirms.

**3 · 方案** [35 s]
The language layer extracts the order, pieces, due date and constraint, asks when something is unclear, and declines what the data cannot answer. The model only proposes: code checks every extracted value against the message text.
Everything numeric happens in the kernel, which has no LLM: eligibility, two delivery dates per shop, and a ranking by an objective that is a setting. The dispatcher confirms before anything is booked.

**4 · 价值** [30 s]
We replayed 120 orders through the shared simulator. Picking workshops at random returns 34 percent of batches late; our lateness setting returns 1.7 percent, and mean turnaround falls from 22 to 9 days, for about 3 percent more cost. On the morning inbox, 17 of 30 messages cannot simply be dispatched. A chat thread lets those through; the desk stops them.

**5 · 进度** [35 s]
The whole loop runs. 56 tests pass, and every number in every reply traces to a tool output. The interface puts the tightest requests first and confirms the safe ones in one click. All six of us labelled the requests independently: kappa 0.70. And we measured the language model: the cheapest one got 86 percent of behaviours right; with a sharper prompt and code checks, 100 percent on these thirty, which we developed on.

**6 · 主目标与发现** [45 s]
Our primary objective is minimise lateness. On the official 120 orders a simple earliest-finish rule ties us at 1.7 percent late. But earliest-finish is greedy: it spends the fast, flexible shops on batches that had slack, and the next tight batch has nowhere to go. Our rule keeps the shops that make the date even if the batch is reworked, takes the least flexible of them, and only then looks at speed. That halves the late days on the official data. On order books the rule was never shaped on, it wins: 0.9 percent late against 1.7 on a hundred jittered copies, and likewise with extra accessory orders, tighter due dates, and Sundays off. The simple rule is still a day faster on average.

**7 · 风险与假设** [30 s]
Four risks. Our labels are frozen, but five needed a team vote, so we state the rule we used. On the official data we only tie the simple rule, so we show both the tie and the stressed results. The cheap language model misreads messages; code now checks what it extracts, and the real test is an inbox our teammates still have to write. And the simulator is a what-if replay. On the right, our assumptions: above all, a confirmed dispatch occupies capacity for later requests.

**8 · 路线图** [30 s]
The plan has four steps; the two ahead of us matter most. First the evidence: a held-out inbox written by teammates who do not touch the prompt, a user test of the interface, and detecting a closed workshop from a stuck queue. Then a public demo and the evaluation. Our criteria are on the bottom row: under 2 percent late, beating the simple rule on fresh data, 90 percent right behaviour on the held-out inbox, and every promised rework date kept.

## 新增问答（v2 的内容会引出的问题）

**Q：官方数据上你们只是打平，凭什么说规则更好？**
A: On the official orders both rules are late on the same two batches, two 2,000-piece accessory orders; ours is late by half as many days. The difference shows when the order book changes: on 100 jittered copies generated after the rule was fixed, ours is better on 72, equal on 25, worse on 3. We also say where the simple rule wins: it is about one day faster on average and has a lower defective share. (附录 A)

**Q：那些“新数据”是不是挑过的？**
A: No. The rule was explored on the official orders, two stress scenarios and 30 jittered sets with seeds 9000 to 9029. The validation sets use seeds from 20000, with different jitter ranges, and were generated after the rule was fixed. We report all of them, including the 3 where we lose.

**Q：1.7% 是不是已经到底了？**
A: No. An exhaustive search with hindsight reaches zero late accessory orders. No rule we tried that does not know the future gets there. We do not claim the floor.

**Q：语言模型在 30 条上 100%，这有意义吗？**
A: Not as a test score: we developed on those 30, and we say so on the slide. On 38 new messages the cheap model gets 97 percent and the larger one 100, but those messages are AI-written and two of our fixes came from errors seen on them. The clean number will come from a held-out inbox written by teammates who do not touch the prompt, frozen before the first run. (附录 C)

**Q：为什么不直接换更好的模型？**
A: We measured both. GPT-5 mini is better out of the box and costs about four times as much, still under four cents per 30 messages. The code check is worth keeping with any model: it drops any value the message does not contain, so the model cannot invent an order number or a quantity.

**Q：便宜模型还会错在哪？**
A: One phrasing: “they want it sooner than planned but haven't said when”. GPT-5 nano does not read that as a pending date and dispatches on the old one, three runs out of three. We did not patch it, because it comes from the test messages; it stays on our failure list.

**Q：为什么要报两个日期？**
A: In 1,200 simulated batches the expected date is kept 94 percent of the time; every miss is a defect rework. The “if reworked” date is kept 100 percent of the time, 99.2 with a workshop closed, and it is 1.8 days later on average. (附录 D)

**Q：你们改了模拟器？**
A: The official results use the simulator unchanged. Separately, we ran a variant with no work on Sundays, because the Factory Primer says workshops run Monday to Saturday. With the Sunday rule switched off, the variant reproduces the official simulator exactly. Under it the simple rule goes from 1.7 to 3.7 percent late and ours to 2.1. We report it in a separate table. (附录 D)

**Q：一键确认会不会让人不看就点？**
A: It only takes recommendations that stay on time even if the batch is reworked, and it holds back any request that shares a workshop with a tight one. On the morning inbox that is 7 of 13; the other 6 are left for the dispatcher, tightest first. We have not tested this with real users yet; that is in the plan.

**Q：你们的标签是怎么定的？分歧大吗？**
A: All six of us classified the 30 messages on our own, kappa 0.70. Twenty-five followed the majority. Five fell short of five out of six, mostly messages that name a workshop which cannot take the job; we settled those by a team vote on one rule: if the named workshop cannot take it, refuse and offer an alternative, even when the message is a question. The counts per kind then match the instructor's published mix exactly.
